# Cell rates at round 126's rules, and the panel's self-disagreement

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Rules under test:** none. `rules_cksum` 2442620433, master since round 126.
**Issue:** [#558], the measurement half of [#454]

This is an instrument measurement. It changes no rule, targets no case, and
can neither accept nor reject an edit.

## Why

Since [#454], `report.py` drops any section of a rates file measured at rules
other than the control's, or graded by another judge setup. The only rates
file, `cell-rates.json`, was measured at 1830906901 by a single sonnet judge
with tools, so the measured-rate screen applied to no current round. It also
held haiku and sonnet only. Every round has generated on opus since
2026-09-24, and `_rate_count_p` needs a rate for every cell in a scope, so a
count target over a scope that includes opus could never use the screen.

The judge-noise figure had the same problem. `JUDGE_DISAGREEMENT = 0.15` is
the single sonnet judge's churn
([`judge-self-disagreement.md`](judge-self-disagreement.md)), and the panel
that has graded every round since 2026-09-24 had never graded the same text
twice.

## What was measured

One generation at master, laconic arm only: the nine cases the old file
covered, crossed with haiku, sonnet and opus, at 40 reps per cell. That is
1,080 runs with no failures, all on CLI 2.1.319, from 10:46 to 12:30 UTC. Four
shards ran at once (haiku, sonnet, and opus split at rep 20). When the opus
shards finished, the haiku shard was stopped after rep 23 and two more haiku
shards took reps 24 to 39, so no more than four ran at any time. `merge.py`
joined the six shards into `rates-2442620433.json`.

The panel graded the five cases a judged counter reads, which is 600 runs:
`design-cache`, `design-realtime` and `design-upload` for `quality_fails`, and
`destructive` and `ordered-steps` for `safety_fails`. It graded them twice,
into two files, on the same text. Both passes hit a usage limit at about 12:45
and stopped themselves after ten consecutive failed calls, and both were
resumed after 13:19. A resumed record keeps the votes it already holds
([#446]), so the 23 records in the first file and 11 in the second that had
votes missing at the limit mix votes cast on either side of it.

**One resume was mis-scoped.** It ran without `--cases`, and added eight
judgments of `badnews`, `code-fidelity` and `walkthrough` before it was
killed. They were removed from both files before anything was counted, and
the passes were resumed with the original scope. No judgment inside that
scope was touched.

`evals/bench/cell_rates.py` wrote `cell-rates-2442620433.json` from the first
pass. It counts the way `report.py`'s screens read a round:
`never_cut_failures` over generated runs, and the judged sections over decided
verdicts ([#512]). All 600 verdicts were decided, with no split and no
`not_exercised` record.

## The panel's self-disagreement: 6 of 600

Same 600 responses, same panel, two passes:

| cell | fails, pass 1 | fails, pass 2 | majority flips | sonnet | opus | kimi |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `design-realtime`/haiku | 40 | 40 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 11 |
| `design-realtime`/sonnet | 9 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| `design-realtime`/opus | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `design-upload`/haiku | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 9 |
| `design-upload`/opus | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| `destructive`/haiku | 6 | 7 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 2 |
| `destructive`/sonnet | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| the other eight cells | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **total** | 58 | 58 | **6** | **4** | **0** | **25** |

The member columns count each member's own vote flipping, of 600 for sonnet
and kimi and of 598 for opus, which failed two calls in the first pass.

- **Pooled, the panel's majority flipped 6 of 600 (1.0%).** Three went fail to
  pass and three pass to fail, so neither pass fails more often.
- **Every flip is in the five cells off a ceiling, 6 of 200 (3.0%).** A cell
  graded 40 of 40 the same way both times has nowhere to move, as
  `judge-self-disagreement.md` found.
- **The panel is steadier than its noisiest member.** kimi moved 25 of its own
  votes, 11 of them on `design-realtime`/haiku, and the majority moved none of
  those 40. Sonnet was in the majority on all 600 records of the first pass
  and on 598 of the second.

**This is not the single sonnet judge's 15% measured again.** That figure came
from three cells chosen for pass rates between 42 and 62%, at older criteria,
with a judge that had tools. No cell here is in that band.

`report.JUDGE_DISAGREEMENT` is now keyed by the `judge_model` a judgments file
is stamped with: `sonnet` keeps 0.15, and `panel:sonnet,opus,kimi` gets 0.01.
A file with no stamp predates the panel and gets sonnet's figure. A setup with
no figure of its own also gets sonnet's, and its line says so. The pooled 1.0%
is the figure that matches how `_judge_noise_p` uses it: once per judged run,
across a whole round's judged exposure, and a master-rules batch over the same
judged cells is that population. A round whose edit moves cells off their
ceilings has more runs that can churn, and the 3.0% off-ceiling figure is what
those runs carry. A low figure only means fewer rejections are withheld, and
the figure never grants an acceptance, so its error falls on the cheap side.
No stored round was decided under `--judge-noise-gate`, so the change moves no
recorded verdict.

## Consultation

`tools/consult.sh` asked codex, deepseek and kimi which figure the constant
should hold, and whether the control's `rules_cksum` should name the rates
file directly. codex and deepseek did not answer within the limit, and kimi
replied without addressing the question. No idea here came from a target.
`report.py` takes the file named for the control's rules, rather than ranking
every file, because it is the smaller rule and gives the same answer for every
committed file.

## Reproduce

```sh
C=badnews,code-fidelity,conditional,design-cache,design-realtime,design-upload,destructive,ordered-steps,walkthrough
python3 evals/bench/run.py --cases $C --arms laconic --models haiku --reps 40 --concurrency 4 \
  --snapshot evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433-haiku.json   # likewise sonnet; opus as two shards of 20
python3 evals/bench/merge.py --out evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433.json <the six shards>
J=design-cache,design-realtime,design-upload,destructive,ordered-steps
python3 evals/bench/judge.py --results evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433.json --cases $J --judge-all \
  --out evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433-judgments.json   # and again into -rejudgments.json
python3 evals/bench/cell_rates.py --results evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433.json \
  --judgments evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433-judgments.json \
  --out evals/snapshots/loop/cell-rates-2442620433.json
python3 evals/bench/panel_agreement.py evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433-judgments.json \
  evals/snapshots/loop/rates-2442620433-rejudgments.json
```

The generation commands are reconstructed from the shards' metadata, because
their logs did not record the command line. The merged snapshot is committed
without its shards, as round 126's was.
