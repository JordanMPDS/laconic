# All three levels — 2026-07-31

The plugin ships `lite`, `full` and `ultra`, and until this run had measured one. Issue #8
asked for the cheapest strong result available: a monotonic ladder across the three, on
both models, with the never-cut check applied at each level.

**The ladder is not there.** `full` is not shorter than `lite`, and `ultra` is not reliably
shorter than `full`. The rule text is cumulative and distinct; the output length is not.

The other three arms were not re-run: `baseline`, `terse-control` and `word-compression`
do not vary with level. `full` was regenerated rather than reused from
[2026-07-31-benchmark.md](2026-07-31-benchmark.md), because that snapshot predates the
same day's `destructive` rule fix — comparing a new-rules `lite` against an old-rules
`full` would have confounded the ladder with that change. All three levels here share one
rules text and one afternoon.

No judge run. The never-cut check is deterministic, the ladder is arithmetic, and 330
judge calls would have roughly doubled the bill for verdicts nothing here claims.

## Length is measured in words, and why that changed the metric

Every earlier document in `evals/results/` measures length in output tokens. That number
counts the model's tool turns as well as its answer, and on a per-case comparison between
levels the tool turns dominate:

| `destructive` / sonnet | lite | full | ultra |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| median output tokens | 1150 | 3268 | 2927 |
| median words of the answer | 215 | 204 | 208 |

The answer is the same length at all three levels. The token column moves by a factor of
three. Between arms at one level that noise is common to every arm and roughly cancels;
between levels it *is* the comparison, so this document leads with words of the response
text and reports output tokens second. Both are published, as is the house convention.

## The result, per boundary

Direction of the change, counted per case and model — 22 cells, two-sided exact sign test:

| boundary | metric | shorter | of | p |
| --- | --- | --: | --: | --: |
| `lite` to `full` | words | 11 | 22 | 1.00 |
| `full` to `ultra` | words | 12 | 22 | 0.83 |
| `lite` to `full` | output tokens | 8 | 22 | 0.29 |
| `full` to `ultra` | output tokens | 16 | 22 | 0.05 |

**`lite` to `full` does nothing measurable, and what signal there is points the wrong
way.** On words the split is exactly even. On tokens, 14 of 22 cells got *longer* at
`full`. The aggregate agrees: median-of-per-case-medians words go 95 to 105 on haiku and
92 to 123 on sonnet — up, at both models, at the boundary where the rule text adds four
prohibitions.

**`full` to `ultra` compresses tokens, weakly, and does not move the answer's word
count.** 16 of 22 cells shorter on tokens (p = 0.05) against 12 of 22 on words (p = 0.83).
The most defensible reading is that `ultra` shortens the model's working, not its reply.

Aggregate, words of the answer, median of per-case medians:

| level | haiku | sonnet |
| --- | --: | --: |
| `lite` | 95 | 92 |
| `full` | 105 | 123 |
| `ultra` | 90 | 94 |

Both models, both estimators, both metrics give **broken** — no ordering of the three
levels holds — except output tokens on haiku under one of the two estimators, which is the
kind of single-cell result this project has already retracted claims for.

## `ultra`'s fallback is doing its job, and it is not what broke the ladder

The rule says `ultra` does not apply on a turn whose honest answer does not fit in a line
or two. Seven of the eleven cases ask for something that cannot: a walkthrough, four
rotation steps, a runnable command with its flags, a diagnosis with a mechanism. On those,
a flat cell is the rule working.

That was anticipated in issue #8, so the four short-answer cases were checked as a
subgroup: `badnews`, `conditional`, `decision`, `floor`, 8 cells. `ultra` is shorter than
`full` in 5 of them, tied in 1, longer in 2. This is a post-hoc subgroup at n=5 and it does
not separate either — but it does mean the fallback is not what is hiding a ladder. The
one large, clean movement in the whole run is `conditional` on sonnet: 92 words at `lite`,
73 at `full`, 27 at `ultra`.

All twelve are arrows in prose, and they land in exactly the two constructions the rule
names as wrong — the runbook line and the mapping:

> **Recommended order:** dump/backup if needed → `DROP TABLE users CASCADE` (or drop
> `invoices`/`sessions` first) → re-run `schema.sql` in full → insert fixtures

> Keep a small registry (or JWKS endpoint if you're on RS256/ES256) mapping `kid` → public
> key/secret.

The arrow prohibition sits above the first level marker, so `lite` carries it verbatim,
identically to `full` and `ultra`. Whatever is happening is not a missing rule.

Two things this is and is not:

- **It is a real limit on a published claim.** "Laconic is the only arm in the run with no
  readability violations" is true of the `full` level, which is what that run measured. It
  does not hold at `lite`.
- **It is not a demonstrated difference between the levels.** The twelve violations are
  concentrated in 3 responses of the 55 sonnet responses at `lite`, against 0 of 55 at
  `full`. Fisher's exact two-sided p = 0.24. Three responses is not a rate.

Filed as its own issue rather than fixed here, because the fix is a rule change and this
run is the measurement, not the diagnosis.

## A cross-check the word metric made cheap

Recomputing the published four-arm comparison in words of the answer, from the committed
snapshot, offline — median of per-case medians:

| model | baseline | terse-control | word-compression | laconic | laconic vs baseline |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| haiku | 96 | 116 | 108 | 101 | +5% |
| sonnet | 183 | 148 | 169 | 100 | **-45%** |

The headline compression figure of 33% on Sonnet was measured in output tokens. Measured in
the words a person actually reads, it is 45% — the token metric was understating it,
because laconic's arm spends tokens on tool turns like every other arm. The Haiku
non-result is unchanged in sign: 1% longer in tokens, 5% longer in words.

This is a recomputation of a committed snapshot, not a new run. It changes no published
number in [2026-07-31-benchmark.md](2026-07-31-benchmark.md); it says what a second metric
gives on the same responses.

## What this run does not show

1. **One arm.** No baseline was generated per level, so nothing here compares a level
   against no rules at all. The ladder is a within-treatment question and that is all it
   answers.
2. **n=5 per cell, 11 cases.** A boundary effect smaller than the case-to-case spread would
   not show. The sign tests bound this: an effect present in 16 of 22 cells would have been
   detected at p = 0.05, and one in 11 of 22 is indistinguishable from nothing.
3. **The judge did not run.** No trap verdicts, so no claim about whether `ultra` stays
   *correct* while it gets shorter. The never-cut check is the only correctness instrument
   applied here, and it covers 45% of responses.
4. **Two models, one vendor**, as with every other run in this directory.
5. **The levels are cumulative in text and were verified as such** — the injected payload
   grows 4205, 4716, 5177 characters across the three, and each level's checksum is
   recorded above. This run says the output length does not follow that structure, not that
   the structure is absent from the prompt.
